# Seller subscriptions and pro tokens

The seller dashboard reads the account's purchased, unexpired subscription and its token balance. Available plans are never treated as an active subscription. Returning to the dashboard or focusing the window reloads these values through the existing resource hook.

Each paid package records its purchased title, role, total pro requests, monthly cap and expiry. Later plan edits or deactivation do not revoke that purchase. Multiple unexpired purchases retain their unused tokens; the earliest expiring eligible package is consumed first. A monthly cap of zero adds no monthly restriction to the finite package total. Monthly usage resets at the calendar-month boundary of the database clock. Expired package tokens are excluded. The dashboard shows both total remaining tokens and, when lower, the allowance currently available within the monthly cap.

Token-only purchases use successful purchase transactions as the credit ledger and successful purchased-token usages as debits. Pending, failed or rejected payments add no credit. Bank callback replay cannot create another transaction credit. Purchased top-up tokens do not require an active package and do not expire with a package. This avoids a separate mutable balance that could diverge from the financial ledger.

Public product pro requests are free for customers, require no customer account and have no personal/IP trial allowance. Every successful try-on consumes one token from that product's seller: eligible package tokens first, then purchased top-up tokens. Empty store credit never falls back to a free trial. Public quota responses return null personal limits and disclose store availability and subscription expiry without exposing the seller's financial ledger or token balance. The API derives the billed seller from the validated product. It binds each uploaded image to that product and the nearest trusted client IP, and consumes an upload only once. Historical trial rows are retained without retroactive charges. The photo provider's endpoints and person/cloth payload remain unchanged.

An account row lock serializes store quota consumption across IP addresses. Customer requests do not acquire a personal trial lock. Upload consumption, usage insertion, and notification are committed together; a failed write rolls back the token debit and leaves the upload retryable. Wallet subscription purchase commits the wallet debit, transaction, purchased entitlement and notification together. Receipt approval and bank settlement use the same subscription activation logic. A unique transaction constraint prevents duplicate subscription activation. Historical User billing routes remain unmounted; customers are never sent to a login or payment screen from public try-on.

The additive entitlement migration snapshots existing subscriptions without extending expiry or duplicating them. Existing successful token purchases are included by the ledger query. Existing non-trial account usages are linked to their original subscription when one can be identified. Back up the production database before deployment, retain all financial records during rollback, and keep the new columns if reverting the application. No pending payment is promoted or settled by the migration.

It covers purchased dashboard data, unlimited customer usage within the store balance, zero customer charges, wallet rollback, plan snapshots, concurrent consumption, token purchases across payment methods, monthly limits/expiry, upload ownership/replay and repeatable legacy migration. ## Administrator revenue reporting

Company income is the sum of settled seller package/token purchases, in toman. Direct gateway payments must also have a successful gateway record and a bank verification timestamp. Pending/failed attempts and unverified success records are excluded. Wallet top-ups are reported separately and are not earned revenue; the later package/token purchase is counted once. No synthetic company_income transaction is inserted to repair the report. Existing ledger rows are read directly, so historical confirmed purchases appear immediately. The administrator income card, chart and company report use the same predicate. The seller payment report includes attempts, wallet funding, settlement status and bank reference, with totals over the complete filtered dataset rather than just one page. The UI has no customer payment tab. Excel exports the full filtered report, with a 20,000-row cap that requires a narrower range rather than silently truncating it.

Run `node --test test/integration/financial.test.js` under the same isolated-database guard. It checks confirmed versus unverified bank payments, pending/failed attempts, wallet funding versus revenue, pagination/export totals, inclusive end dates, administrator-only access and preservation of income when a seller is blocked.
